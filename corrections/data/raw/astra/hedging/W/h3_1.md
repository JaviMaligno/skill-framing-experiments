**Before buying demolition tools, establish whether the wall is load-bearing and whether it contains wiring, plumbing or gas pipes.** You can’t reliably judge that by tapping it or checking its thickness. If its structural role isn’t confirmed, don’t start knocking it down this weekend—removing it could cause a collapse.

Your tool list depends on the wall:

- **Plasterboard partition:** utility knife, pry bar, claw hammer and drill/driver.
- **Brick or block:** club hammer, masonry bolster and possibly a demolition hammer—but only once any required structural support is designed and installed.
- **For either:** safety goggles, suitable dust respirator, hearing protection, tough gloves, safety boots, dust sheets, dust extraction and rubble bags.

A cable-and-pipe detector helps, but a clear reading doesn’t prove the wall is safe to cut. Avoid swinging a sledgehammer or using a reciprocating saw into an unopened wall.
