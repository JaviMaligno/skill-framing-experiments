**Before buying demolition tools, establish whether the wall is load-bearing and whether it contains wiring, plumbing or gas pipes.** You can’t reliably tell from tapping it or its thickness. If that hasn’t been checked, make this weekend an inspection weekend—not a demolition weekend. Removing structural support can cause a collapse.

Once it’s confirmed as a non-load-bearing partition:

1. Check what’s inside and have affected services safely isolated or rerouted. A detector helps, but doesn’t prove the wall is clear.
2. Protect floors, seal doorways against dust and plan rubble removal.
3. Remove the wall covering in small sections before dismantling the exposed framing. Don’t start by swinging a sledgehammer or cutting blindly.

**Tools and materials:** safety goggles, suitable dust respirator, gloves, safety boots, heavy-duty floor protection, dust sheeting, utility knife, pry bar, claw hammer, service detector, rubble bags and a suitable dust extractor. Masonry needs different demolition tools from a timber-and-plasterboard partition.

Is the wall brick/block or plasterboard, and has anyone checked whether it supports anything above?
