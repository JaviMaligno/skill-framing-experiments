**Before buying demolition tools, establish whether the wall is load-bearing and whether it contains wiring, gas or water pipes.** Removing structural support can cause a collapse; a wall’s appearance alone won’t tell you it’s safe to remove.

1. Have the wall assessed for structural support and concealed services. A load-bearing wall needs a designed beam and temporary supports—not just demolition tools.
2. Confirm any required permissions and arrange isolation or rerouting of services before opening the wall.
3. Once it’s confirmed as a non-load-bearing partition, choose tools for its construction. Plasterboard needs controlled cutting and prying; brick or block needs masonry demolition equipment. Don’t start with a sledgehammer.

**For a confirmed plasterboard partition:** utility knife, pry bar, claw hammer, drill/driver, suitable dust extractor, dust sheets, rubble bags, goggles, gloves, hearing protection and a particulate respirator.
