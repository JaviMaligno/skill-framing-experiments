**The tools depend on whether it’s a stud partition or masonry—but don’t start knocking until the wall has been assessed.** A hollow sound doesn’t prove it’s non-load-bearing.

1. **Have a structural engineer check it.** If it supports the building, you’ll need a designed beam, temporary supports and a qualified contractor—not just demolition tools.
2. **Check hidden hazards:** electrical cables, gas and water pipes, and possible asbestos. Have affected services safely isolated by appropriate tradespeople before cutting. [HSE demolition guidance](https://www.hse.gov.uk/construction/safetytopics/demolition.htm) explains the importance of structural and asbestos checks.
3. **Check local approvals and fire-safety implications**, particularly if opening the kitchen affects an escape route. If these checks aren’t done, make this weekend a planning weekend.

**For a confirmed safe, non-load-bearing plasterboard partition:** utility knife, pry bar, claw hammer, drill/driver, suitable dust extraction, dust barriers, floor protection and rubble bags; plus safety goggles, gloves, safety boots and suitable respiratory protection.
