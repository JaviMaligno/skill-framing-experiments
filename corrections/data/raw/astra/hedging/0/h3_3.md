**The tools depend on the wall’s construction—but don’t start knocking it down until its structural role and hidden services are checked.** A hollow sound doesn’t prove it’s safe to remove.

1. **Have a structural engineer assess it.** If it supports the building, you’ll need a designed support arrangement and a qualified contractor—not just demolition tools. [HSE structural safety guidance](https://www.hse.gov.uk/construction/safetytopics/buildings.htm).
2. **Check what’s inside:** electrical cables, gas and water pipes, and potentially asbestos-containing materials. Have relevant trades isolate or relocate services before demolition.
3. **Check local approvals and fire-safety implications**, particularly whether opening the kitchen affects an escape route. If these checks aren’t complete, use this weekend for planning.

**For a confirmed, cleared non-structural plasterboard partition**, typical tools and materials are:
- Utility knife, pry bar, claw hammer and drill/driver.
- Safety goggles, gloves, safety boots, hearing protection and suitable dust respirator.
- Dust barriers, floor protection, appropriate dust extraction and rubble bags.
