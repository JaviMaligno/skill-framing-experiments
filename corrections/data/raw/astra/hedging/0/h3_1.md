**The tools depend on the wall—but don’t start knocking it down until it’s been assessed.** Even a stud wall can support weight.

1. **Get a structural engineer to check it.** If it’s load-bearing, you’ll need a designed support arrangement and a competent contractor—not just demolition tools.
2. **Check hidden hazards.** Locate electricity, gas and water services, and have affected services safely isolated. Have suspect materials assessed for asbestos before disturbing them. [HSE demolition guidance](https://www.hse.gov.uk/construction/safetytopics/demolition.htm) explains these planning risks.
3. **Check local approvals and fire-safety requirements.** Opening a kitchen into a living room can affect escape routes. If these checks aren’t complete, postpone demolition this weekend.

**For a confirmed non-load-bearing plasterboard partition**, typical supplies include a utility knife, pry bar, claw hammer, drill/driver, service detector, floor protection, dust barriers, suitable dust extraction, rubble bags, safety goggles, gloves, safety boots and suitable respiratory protection. A detector alone cannot prove it’s safe to cut.
