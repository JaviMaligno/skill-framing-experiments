On walls, put a screw about every **300 mm (12 in)** along each stud. On ceilings, go closer, about every **230 mm (9 in)**, because the board's weight pulls down on the fixings.

**Tips for a solid fix:**
1. **Keep away from the edges.** Set screws about 10 mm from the paper-covered long edges and about 13 mm from cut ends so the board doesn't crumble.
2. **Screw into every stud** the board crosses, including the middle ones, not just at the joins.
3. **Sink each head just below the surface.** The paper should dimple slightly but not tear. A torn screw hole won't hold, so add another screw close by.
4. **Work from the centre outwards** so the board sits flat against the studs.
5. Use screws long enough to go **at least 25 mm into the timber**. For 12.5 mm board, that usually means 32–38 mm screws.

Local building rules or the board maker may ask for closer spacing, especially for fire-rated or moisture-resistant boards. Check the pack.

**You'll need:**
- Drywall screws (coarse thread for timber studs)
- A cordless drill/driver, ideally with a depth-stop drywall bit
- A tape measure and pencil
- A spirit level
