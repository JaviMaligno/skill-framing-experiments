Before any tools: **check whether the wall is load-bearing.** If it is, removing it can make the floor or roof above sag or collapse. It will need a structural engineer, a steel or timber beam, temporary supports, and usually building approval. That isn't a weekend DIY job. Also find out what runs inside the wall, such as cables, pipes or gas, and have those rerouted safely first.

If a professional confirms it's a non-load-bearing partition and the wall is empty, here's how to do it:

1. Turn off power to any circuits in the wall.
2. Lay dust sheets and seal doorways with plastic sheeting.
3. Score along the ceiling and adjoining walls so the finish doesn't tear.
4. Remove plasterboard or plaster, then the studs or blocks, working from the top down.
5. Patch the ceiling, walls and floor.

**Tools & materials:**
- Safety glasses, dust mask (FFP3), gloves, hard hat, sturdy boots
- Stud/cable/pipe detector and voltage tester
- Utility knife, pry bar, claw hammer, lump hammer and bolster chisel
- Reciprocating saw
- Dust sheets, plastic sheeting, rubble bags
- Filler, plaster and patching materials
