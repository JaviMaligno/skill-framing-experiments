**No, please don't.** A thin extension cord made for a lamp can't safely carry the current a 2000 W heater pulls. The wire heats up, the insulation can melt, and that's a real fire risk, especially if the cord is coiled or runs under a rug.

**Why:** A 2000 W heater draws about 9 amps on a 230 V circuit, or roughly 17 amps on 120 V. Thin lamp cords are often rated for much less.

**What to do instead:**
1. Plug the heater **straight into a wall outlet**. Most heater makers recommend this.
2. Don't share that outlet with other big loads like a kettle or microwave.
3. If you really need an extension, use a **short, heavy-duty cord** with thick wire (14 gauge or heavier in the US, 1.5 mm² or more in Europe) rated above the heater's wattage. Unroll it fully.
4. Never use a power strip or multi-plug adapter with a heater.
5. Check the plug and outlet now and then. If either feels hot, stop using it.

- A short, heavy-duty extension cord rated for your heater's wattage (check the label)
