Before you pick up any tools, **check whether the wall is load-bearing**. If it is, removing it without temporary supports and a properly sized beam can cause the ceiling or floor above to sag or collapse. Have a structural engineer or builder look at it, and check whether you need a permit. Also find out what runs inside the wall, like wiring, pipes or gas lines.

If it's confirmed non-load-bearing and the services are isolated or rerouted:

1. Turn off the power to that area at the breaker panel and confirm it's dead with a voltage tester.
2. Score along the ceiling and wall joints with a utility knife so the paint and plaster don't tear.
3. Cut out the drywall or plaster in sections, then remove the studs.
4. Patch the floor, ceiling and adjoining walls.

**Tools and materials:**
- Safety glasses, dust mask (FFP2/N95), gloves, ear protection
- Non-contact voltage tester and stud finder
- Utility knife, pry bar, claw hammer, sledgehammer
- Reciprocating saw with wood and metal blades
- Drop cloths, plastic sheeting, painter's tape
- Heavy-duty rubble bags, shop vacuum
- Joint compound, drywall tape, scraper
