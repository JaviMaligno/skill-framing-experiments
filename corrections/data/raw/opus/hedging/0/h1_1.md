**No, don't do that.** A thin lamp cord is usually rated for about 5–10 amps. A 2000 W heater pulls around 8–9 amps on 230 V and about 16–17 amps on 120 V, and it does that for hours at a time. That load can overheat a light-duty cord, melt the insulation and start a fire.

**What to do instead:**

1. **Plug the heater straight into a wall outlet.** This is what most heater manufacturers recommend, and it's the safest choice.
2. **Don't use a power strip or multi-plug adapter** for the heater either.
3. **If you really need an extension**, use a short, heavy-duty cord with a current rating at or above what the heater draws (16 A or more). It should have thick wire, roughly 1.5 mm² or 12–14 AWG.
4. **Unroll the cord all the way** and keep it out from under rugs and furniture so heat can escape.
5. **Check the plug after a while.** If it feels warm, unplug it, because that means something is overloaded.

**If you need a cord:** a heavy-duty, short appliance extension cord with a high current rating.
