**Go with satin for a hallway.** Hallways get a lot of wear: bags, shoulders, hands and the vacuum bump into the walls all the time. A satin finish is tougher and easier to wipe clean, so scuffs and fingermarks come off without leaving a shiny patch.

**When matt still makes sense:**
- Walls that are uneven or patched. Matt hides flaws, while satin's slight sheen shows them up.
- You prefer a soft, flat look. If so, choose a **durable or scrubbable matt** (it'll say "washable" or "scrubbable" on the tin).

**A good compromise:**
1. Use satin or eggshell (a low sheen, between matt and satin) on the walls, or at least on the lower half if you have a dado rail.
2. Use satin or gloss on the woodwork, like skirting boards, door frames and doors.
3. Use matt on the ceiling, where nothing touches it.

**What you'll need:**
- Satin or scrubbable matt emulsion (water-based wall paint)
- Satin or gloss paint for the woodwork
- Filler, sandpaper and a sugar soap solution for prep
- Roller, tray, angled brush, masking tape and dust sheets
