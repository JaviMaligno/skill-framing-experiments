Before buying anything, **find out whether the wall is load-bearing.** If it holds up floors or the roof, taking it out without temporary supports and a properly sized beam can make the ceiling sag or collapse. Ask a structural engineer or builder to check it, and check with your local building department about permits.

If the wall is confirmed **non-load-bearing**, here's how to do it:

1. Turn off power to that wall at the breaker and check for live wires with a voltage tester. Look for pipes and ducts too, and have an electrician or plumber reroute anything you find.
2. Seal the doorways with plastic sheeting and cover the floors.
3. Cut out the plasterboard in sections, then remove the studs.
4. Patch the ceiling, walls and floor where the wall used to be.

If your home is older, get the wall tested for asbestos or lead paint first.

**Tools and materials:**
- Stud finder and voltage tester
- Utility knife, pry bar and claw hammer
- Reciprocating saw
- Sledgehammer, for plaster or lath walls
- Safety glasses, a dust mask (FFP2/N95), gloves and ear protection
- Plastic sheeting, tape and floor protection
- Heavy rubble bags, a shop vacuum and a stepladder
