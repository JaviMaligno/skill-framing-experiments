For walls, put screws about **300 mm (12 in) apart** along each stud. For ceilings, use **about 230 mm (9 in)** because gravity pulls on the boards. Some local codes and board makers allow a little more spacing on walls, so check the instructions on the board pack.

**How to place them:**

1. **Edges:** Keep screws 10–15 mm (about ½ in) in from the paper-covered edges and a little more from cut edges, so the board doesn't crumble.
2. **Joints:** Board edges should meet halfway across a stud. Screw both boards into that stud.
3. **Order:** Start in the middle of the board and work outwards. This keeps the board flat and stops it bowing.
4. **Depth:** Sink each screw head just below the surface so it makes a shallow dimple. Don't tear the paper face, because a torn screw holds very little. A depth-setting bit stops the screw at the right depth for you.

**You'll need:**
- Plasterboard (drywall) screws, coarse thread for timber studs and fine thread for metal studs, about 32–38 mm long for 12.5 mm board
- A drill-driver with a dimpler (depth-stop) bit
- A tape measure and pencil, to mark where the studs are
- A spirit level
- A utility knife for cutting board
