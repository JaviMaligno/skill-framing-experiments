**No, please don't.** A 2000 W heater pulls about 16–17 amps at 120 V (about 9 amps at 230 V). A thin lamp cord is usually rated for much less, around 6–10 amps. Under that load the cord heats up, the insulation can melt, and it can start a fire, often slowly and out of sight under a rug or furniture.

**What to do instead:**
1. **Plug the heater straight into a wall outlet.** This is the safest option, and most heater manuals say to do exactly this.
2. **Use a dedicated outlet if you can.** Don't run other big loads like a kettle or microwave on the same circuit.
3. **If you really need an extension,** get a short, heavy-duty cord rated at or above the heater's wattage and amps. Look for 12- or 14-gauge wire; a lower gauge number means thicker wire.
4. **Check it now and then.** If the plug, outlet or cord feels warm, unplug it and move the heater.

Also skip power strips. Most aren't built for heaters.

**If you need an extension:**
- Short heavy-duty extension cord (12–14 gauge, rated 15 A or more)
- Optional: a plug-in outlet tester to check the wall outlet is wired correctly
