**For a hallway, go with satin (or eggshell, which is a bit less shiny).**

Hallways get a lot of wear: hands on the walls, bags and shoulders brushing past, scuffs from shoes and vacuum cleaners. Satin has a slight sheen that makes the surface tougher and easy to wipe clean. Matt (flat) paint marks easily, and scrubbing it often leaves a shiny patch.

**When matt still makes sense:**
- Your walls are bumpy or patched. Matt hides flaws, and sheen shows them up.
- You choose a **durable or scrubbable matt**. These are made for busy areas and give you a flat look that you can still clean.

**A common combination:** durable matt or satin on the walls, and satin or semi-gloss on the doors, skirting boards and frames, which take the most knocks.

**What you'll need:**
- Satin or durable matt wall paint
- Filler and fine sandpaper for dents
- Sugar soap or a mild degreaser to clean the walls first
- Painter's tape and drop cloths
- A roller (short pile for satin), a tray and an angled brush for edges
