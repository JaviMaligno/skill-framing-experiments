You'll need a few demolition and dust-control tools. Before you swing anything, though, make sure the wall isn't **load-bearing**, meaning it doesn't hold up the floor or roof above. Signs that it might be: it runs at right angles to the floor joists, it sits under another wall upstairs, or it's an exterior or central spine wall. If it carries load, it needs a properly sized beam and temporary props, and that isn't a weekend job. Also check for wiring, pipes or gas running through it.

For a non-load-bearing stud wall:

1. Turn off the power to any circuits in the wall and check them with a voltage tester.
2. Seal the doorways with plastic sheeting and cover the floors.
3. Cut a small inspection hole first and look inside.
4. Strip off the drywall or plaster, then cut out the studs.
5. Patch the ceiling, floor and side walls where the wall used to be.

**Tools and materials:**
- Non-contact voltage tester and stud finder
- Sledgehammer and pry bar
- Reciprocating saw with demolition blades
- Utility knife and drywall saw
- Safety glasses, dust mask (FFP2/N95), gloves and ear protection
- Plastic sheeting, painter's tape and floor protection
- Heavy-duty rubble bags or a skip
- Joint compound and patching materials
