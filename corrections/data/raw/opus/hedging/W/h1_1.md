**No, don't do it.** A thin lamp cord usually can't handle anywhere near the power a 2000 W heater pulls (around 8–9 amps continuously on a 230 V circuit, or more than 16 amps on 120 V). The wires inside heat up, the insulation softens, and that's how cord fires start, often behind furniture or under a rug where nobody sees it.

**What to do instead:**

1. **Plug the heater straight into a wall outlet.** This is the best option, and most heater manuals say the same.
2. **Need extra reach?** Use a short, heavy-duty extension cord rated at or above the heater's wattage or amperage (check the label on the cord). It should have thick conductors, typically 1.5 mm² / 14 AWG or heavier.
3. **Skip power strips and multi-plug adapters** for the heater, and don't run the cord under rugs.
4. **Check the plug after an hour.** If the plug or outlet feels hot, stop using that setup.

**What you'd need:**
- A heavy-duty extension cord rated for 2000 W or more (only if the wall outlet is out of reach)
