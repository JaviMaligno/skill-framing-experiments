The key question is whether the wall is load-bearing. If it is, it holds up the floor or roof above, and taking it out without temporary supports and a beam can bring the ceiling down. Check this before you buy any tools. Signs that it's load-bearing:
- Joists above it run at right angles to the wall
- There's a wall in the same spot on the floor above
- It's in the middle of the house

If you can't tell, have it checked. Then make sure no cables or pipes run through it, and turn off the power to that area before you start.

**For a non-load-bearing stud wall:**
1. Cover the floor and seal the doorways with plastic sheeting.
2. Cut and pull off the drywall or plaster.
3. Cut out the studs in the middle, then pry the top and bottom plates (the horizontal timbers) away from the ceiling and floor.
4. Patch the ceiling, walls and floor.

**Tools and materials:**
- Safety glasses, dust mask and work gloves
- Stud finder that also detects live wires
- Sledgehammer and a crowbar or pry bar
- Reciprocating saw (a power saw that cuts through wood and nails)
- Utility knife
- Drop cloths, plastic sheeting and heavy rubble bags
- Joint compound and patching materials
