**Walls: screws about 300 mm (12 in) apart along each stud. Ceilings: closer, about 230 mm (9 in), because gravity pulls the board down.**

How to do it:

1. **Edges and middle.** Use the same spacing along the board edges as down the middle studs. Don't use wider gaps in the middle.
2. **Distance from the edge.** Keep screws at least 10 mm in from the factory edges and 13 mm in from cut edges, or the board can crumble.
3. **Screw length.** Get at least 25 mm of thread into the stud. For standard 12.5 mm board on timber, 38 mm screws are a safe choice.
4. **Depth.** Sink each head just below the surface without tearing the paper face. A dimpler bit (a drill bit that stops the screw at the right depth) makes this easy. If a screw tears the paper, put another one about 50 mm away.
5. **Work in order.** Start from the middle of the board and work outwards so it sits flat with no bulges.

**One warning:** before you screw, check behind the board line for pipes and cables, especially straight up and down from sockets and switches. A detector is cheap insurance.

**You'll need:** drywall screws (fine thread for metal studs, coarse for timber), a drill/driver with a dimpler bit, a stud and cable detector, a tape measure and a pencil.
