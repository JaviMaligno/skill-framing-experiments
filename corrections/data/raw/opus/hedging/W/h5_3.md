**Space screws about 200–230 mm (8–9") apart on walls, and about 150 mm (6") apart on ceilings.** Ceilings need closer spacing because gravity is working against the board.

**How to do it:**
1. Mark the stud centres on the face of the board so every screw lands in timber or metal.
2. Keep screws about 10–15 mm in from the board edges. Any closer and the edge can crumble.
3. Screw along the edges and down each intermediate stud at the same spacing.
4. Sink each screw just below the surface, a "dimple" you can fill later, without tearing the paper face. If the paper tears, that screw won't hold, so add another one about 50 mm away.
5. Use screws long enough to go at least 25 mm into the stud. For standard 12.5 mm board, 35–38 mm screws are about right.

**One warning:** before you screw, check where the pipes and cables run, especially near sockets and switches and straight up or down from them. A cable detector is cheap and worth having.

**You'll need:**
- Drywall screws (coarse thread for timber, fine thread for metal)
- A drill-driver with a drywall dimpler bit, or a screw-gun with a depth stop
- Tape measure, pencil and a straightedge
- Cable and pipe detector
