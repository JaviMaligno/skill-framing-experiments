**About 150mm apart around the edges of each board and up to 230mm along the studs in the middle.** On ceilings, use 150mm everywhere, because the board's weight pulls on every screw.

1. **Keep screws 10–15mm in from the board edges.** Closer than that and the edge can crumble.
2. **Use screws long enough to go at least 25mm into the stud.** That's about 38mm screws for 12.5mm board.
3. **Sink each head just below the paper surface without tearing it.** A dimpler bit makes this easy. A torn screw hole has almost no grip.
4. **Where two boards meet on a stud, offset the screws** so you don't split the timber.
5. **Work from the centre of the board outwards** so it sits flat against the studs.

**One warning:** cables and pipes often run through or alongside studs. Run a detector along each stud before you screw, and take care around switches and sockets.

**You'll need:**
- Drywall screws (coarse thread for timber studs)
- A drill-driver with a dimpler bit, or a screw gun with depth control
- A cable and pipe detector
- A tape measure and pencil to mark the stud lines
