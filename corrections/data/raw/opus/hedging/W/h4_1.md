**Go with satin for a hallway.** Hallways get bumped, brushed against and scuffed by bags, shoes and hands, and satin cleans much better. You can wipe off a mark with a damp cloth. On a matt finish, scrubbing usually leaves a shiny patch.

**When matt still makes sense:**
- On the **ceiling**, where nobody touches it and matt hides flaws.
- On **walls with dents or patchy filler**. Satin's slight sheen shows every bump, especially in light coming along the wall. If your walls aren't great, a **durable or scrubbable matt** (a tougher matt made to be washed) gets you most of the cleanability without the shine.
- For **woodwork** like skirting, doors and frames, use satin or gloss. Satin looks a bit more modern.

**Quick tips:**
1. Fill and sand the walls first. Satin shows prep mistakes.
2. Keep a wet edge and finish each wall in one go so you don't get patchy sheen lines.
3. Plan on two coats.

**You'll need:** satin emulsion (or durable matt), filler, a filling knife, fine sandpaper, masking tape, dust sheets, a medium-pile roller and tray, and a 2" angled brush for cutting in.
