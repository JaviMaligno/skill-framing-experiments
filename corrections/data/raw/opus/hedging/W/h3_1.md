Before the tools, find out whether the wall is **load-bearing**. If joists from the floor above rest on it, or it runs down the middle of the house, taking it out without temporary supports and a beam can bring the ceiling down. Removing one also usually needs a structural calculation and building approval. If it's a non-structural partition wall, this weekend is realistic.

**The job, briefly:**
1. Turn off the power to that circuit, then use a detector to scan for cables and pipes. Reroute anything you find before you cut.
2. Clear the room, lay dust sheets and tape plastic over the doorways.
3. Score along the ceiling and adjoining walls so the cut is clean.
4. Remove the plasterboard or plaster, then the studs or blocks, working from the top down.
5. Patch the ceiling, walls and floor where the wall used to be.

**Tools and materials:**
- Cable, pipe and stud detector
- Voltage tester
- Sledgehammer and lump hammer
- Bolster chisel
- Reciprocating saw (or a handsaw for timber studs)
- Pry bar
- Utility knife
- Safety glasses, dust mask (FFP2 or better), gloves
- Dust sheets, plastic sheeting, masking tape
- Heavy-duty rubble sacks
- Filler or plaster, plus a trowel to make good afterwards
