**No, don't do it.** A thin lamp cord is usually rated for about 600–800 W. A 2000 W heater pulls close to its full load for hours, so the cord overheats and can melt or start a fire, often under a rug or behind furniture where you won't see it. This is the one warning that really matters here.

**What to do instead:**

1. **Plug the heater straight into a wall outlet.** This is the best option, and most heater manuals say to do it.
2. **If you really need more reach,** use a short, heavy-duty extension cord rated for at least 15 A / 1800–2000 W (look for 14-gauge or thicker, where a lower gauge number means thicker wire). Keep it as short as you can.
3. **Never use a power strip or a multi-outlet adapter** with a heater.
4. **Run the cord in the open,** not under rugs or furniture.
5. **Check the plug now and then.** If it feels hot, unplug it and move the heater to a different outlet.

**If you need a cord:**
- A heavy-duty extension cord (14 AWG or thicker, rated 15 A, single outlet, as short as possible)
