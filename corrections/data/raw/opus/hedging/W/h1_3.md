**No, don't do it.** A thin lamp cord is the classic way space heaters start house fires. A 2000 W heater pulls about 8–9 amps on 230 V, or around 16+ amps on 120 V. Light-duty cords are made for a fraction of that, so the wire heats up, the insulation melts, and it can catch fire with no warning.

**What to do instead:**

1. **Plug the heater straight into a wall outlet.** Almost every heater manufacturer asks for this, and it's the safest option.
2. **If you really have to use an extension**, get a short, heavy-duty cord rated above the heater's wattage or amperage. That means 14-gauge or thicker (1.5 mm² or more), labeled for appliances or heaters.
3. **Don't use power strips, and don't daisy-chain cords together.**
4. **Uncoil the cord all the way and keep it out from under rugs.** A coiled or covered cord traps heat.
5. **Feel the plug after 15–20 minutes.** If it's more than slightly warm, unplug it.

Your thin cord is fine for the desk lamp.

**If you need an extension:**
- Short heavy-duty appliance extension cord (14 AWG / 1.5 mm² or heavier, rated at least 15 A on 120 V or 10 A on 230 V)
