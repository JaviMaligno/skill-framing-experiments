**Don't mix them.** Bleach and an acid descaler react and release chlorine gas, which can seriously hurt your lungs even in a small bathroom. Mixing doesn't make it stronger anyway. Use the bleach on the mould and keep the descaler for limescale on a different day.

How to do it with bleach alone:

1. Open a window and run the extractor fan.
2. Mix about 1 part bleach to 4 parts water in a spray bottle, or use a ready-made mould spray.
3. Spray the black patches and leave it for **10–15 minutes**.
4. Scrub with an old toothbrush or a grout brush, then rinse with clean water and dry the area.
5. If the stain is still there, spray again and leave it 30 minutes.

If the mould has grown into the silicone sealant, bleach usually won't shift it. Cut the old bead out and run a new line of mould-resistant sealant.

To stop it coming back, run the fan during showers and for 20 minutes after, and wipe down wet surfaces.

**You'll need:** bleach, spray bottle, rubber gloves, grout brush or old toothbrush, cloth. For sealant: a sealant remover tool, mould-resistant silicone and a caulking gun.
