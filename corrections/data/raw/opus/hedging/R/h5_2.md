**Space the screws about 200–230 mm apart along each stud on walls, and about 150 mm apart on ceilings.** Ceilings need them closer because gravity is pulling the board away.

1. **Mark the stud lines.** Draw a faint pencil line on the board so every screw hits the middle of a stud.
2. **Keep screws in from the edges.** Stay about 10 mm in from the paper-covered long edges and 13 mm in from cut ends so the board doesn't crumble.
3. **Fix edges and middles.** Screw along every stud the board crosses, not just the edges where boards join.
4. **Set the depth.** Each screw head should sit just below the surface in a slight dimple without tearing the paper. A torn screw hole won't hold, so add another screw about 50 mm away.
5. **Use the right length.** For standard 12.5 mm board, use 35–38 mm screws so at least 25 mm goes into the timber.

**Important:** Check for hidden cables and pipes with a detector before you screw near studs.

**You'll need:**
- Drywall screws (coarse thread for timber studs)
- A drill-driver with a drywall dimpler bit, or a depth-setting drywall screwdriver
- A tape measure, pencil and long straightedge
- A cable and pipe detector
