First, make sure the wall isn't load-bearing. If it holds up joists or a floor above, taking it out without temporary supports and a beam can bring the ceiling down. Signs to look for: the joists above run across the wall rather than alongside it, there's a wall directly above or below it, or it's an exterior-facing structural line. If any of those apply, you need a structural engineer before you swing a hammer. Also turn off the power to any outlets or switches in the wall and confirm they're dead. If the house is from before the 1980s, test for asbestos and lead paint.

**For a non-load-bearing wall:**

1. Cut the power at the breaker and test every outlet and switch in the wall.
2. Score along the corners and ceiling line so you don't tear the plaster you're keeping.
3. Punch a hole, find the studs, and check for pipes and cables.
4. Strip the drywall or plaster, then cut and lever out the studs.
5. Remove the top and bottom plates and patch the floor, walls and ceiling.

**Tools and materials:**
- Stud finder with wire detection
- Non-contact voltage tester
- Utility knife
- Sledgehammer and claw hammer
- Pry bar
- Reciprocating saw with wood and metal blades
- Safety glasses, dust mask (FFP2/N95), gloves
- Plastic sheeting, painter's tape and heavy rubble bags
- Joint compound and patching plaster
