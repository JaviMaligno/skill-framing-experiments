**No, don't plug it in.** A thin lamp cord can't carry that much current. A 2000 W heater pulls about 9 A on 230 V, or about 17 A on 120 V. Thin cords are usually rated for 6–10 A. An overloaded cord heats up, the insulation softens, and it can start a fire, often under a rug or behind furniture where nobody sees it.

**What to do instead:**

1. **Plug the heater straight into a wall outlet.** This is the best option, and most heater manuals say to do it.
2. **If you really need a cord,** use a short, heavy-duty one rated for the heater's power. Look for at least 16 A / 1.5 mm² wire (or 12–14 AWG in the US), and check the rating printed on the cord or plug.
3. **Unroll the whole cord.** A coiled cord traps heat.
4. **Don't put anything else on that cord or a power strip** while the heater is running.
5. **Check the plug after an hour.** If it's hot to the touch, unplug it and use another outlet.

**What you'll need (only if you can't reach a wall outlet):**
- A short heavy-duty extension cord rated 16 A (15 A in the US) or higher
