The main thing to sort out before the tools is **whether the wall is load-bearing.** If it holds up joists or a wall above, taking it out without a steel or timber beam and temporary props can bring the ceiling down. Signs that it is: joists above run across it rather than alongside it, it sits on the house's central line, or there's a wall directly above it upstairs. If any of those apply, you'll need a structural engineer's beam spec (and usually building approval) before the weekend. Also check for cables and pipes inside it.

If it's a non-structural stud or block partition:

1. Turn off power to any circuits in the wall and reroute sockets or switches.
2. Score along the ceiling and adjoining walls so the finish doesn't tear.
3. Open it up from the top down, a section at a time.
4. Take out the studs or blocks, then the top and bottom plates.
5. Patch the ceiling, walls and floor where the wall stood.

**Tools and materials:**
- Voltage tester and stud/cable detector
- Utility knife
- Sledgehammer and lump hammer
- Pry bar and claw hammer
- Reciprocating saw with wood/metal blades
- Cold chisel (for block walls)
- Safety glasses, dust mask (FFP2 or better), gloves
- Dust sheets, plastic sheeting, rubble bags
- Plasterboard, joint compound, filler for the patch-up
