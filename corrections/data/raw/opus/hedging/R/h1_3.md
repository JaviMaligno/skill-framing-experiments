**No. A thin lamp cord can't safely carry a 2000 W heater.** At about 16–17 amps, the heater will overheat a light-duty cord, and the cord can melt or start a fire, often where it's hidden under a rug or furniture.

**What to do instead:**

1. **Plug the heater straight into a wall outlet.** This is what heater manufacturers recommend, and it's the safest choice.
2. **Check the outlet.** If the plug or outlet feels hot after a while, unplug it. A loose or worn outlet should be replaced.
3. **If you really have to use a cord,** get a short, heavy-duty one rated for at least 15 amps / 1875 W. That means 12- or 14-gauge wire; a lower gauge number means thicker wire. Uncoil it fully, and don't run it under rugs.
4. **Don't use a power strip** for the heater either. Most aren't built for that kind of steady load.

**If you need a cord, you'll need:**
- A heavy-duty extension cord, 12 or 14 gauge, 15 A rated, as short as possible (6 ft or less)
