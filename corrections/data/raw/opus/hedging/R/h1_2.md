**No, don't do it.** A thin lamp cord is usually 16 or 18 gauge and rated for about 7–13 amps. A 2000 W heater pulls around 16–17 amps at 120 V, or about 9 A at 230 V, and it does that for hours at a time. A thin cord carrying that much current heats up, the insulation melts, and it can start a fire, especially if it's coiled or under a rug.

**Best option:** Plug the heater straight into a wall outlet. Most heater manufacturers say to do this, and it's the safest setup.

**If you really need more reach:**
1. Get a heavy-duty extension cord: **12 or 14 gauge** (lower number means thicker wire), rated for at least **15 A / 1800 W**. In a 230 V country, look for 1.5 mm² wire or thicker, rated for at least 10 A.
2. Keep it as short as you can and fully unrolled.
3. Plug nothing else into the same cord or power strip.
4. Keep it out from under rugs and furniture.
5. Check the plug after an hour. If it feels warm, unplug it, because the cord isn't up to the job.

**What you'd need:**
- A short heavy-duty extension cord (12–14 AWG or 1.5 mm²+, single outlet), *or*
- Nothing at all, if you move the heater closer to a wall outlet.
