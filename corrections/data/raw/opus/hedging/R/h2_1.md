**Don't combine them.** Bleach and an acid descaler react and release chlorine gas, which can seriously damage your lungs even in a small bathroom. Mixing them won't make the bleach work better. You only need one product, and for mould that's the bleach.

**How to use the bleach on its own:**
1. Open a window and run the extractor fan.
2. Dilute the bleach, about 1 part bleach to 4 parts water, or use a ready-made mould spray.
3. Spray or brush it onto the mould and leave it for **10–15 minutes**.
4. Scrub with a stiff brush. An old toothbrush works well on grout lines.
5. Rinse with clean water and dry the area completely.

If you want to use the descaler for limescale too, do it on a different day. If it has to be the same day, rinse very thoroughly between the two jobs.

If black stains are still there after two rounds, the mould has usually gone deep into the silicone sealant. Cut the old sealant out and apply new mould-resistant sealant.

**You'll need:** bleach or a mould spray, a spray bottle, a stiff brush, rubber gloves, a cloth, and possibly mould-resistant silicone sealant with a sealant remover tool.
