For walls, put screws about **300 mm (12 in) apart** along each stud and about **200 mm (8 in) apart** along the edges of the board. Ceilings need them closer, around **230 mm (9 in)** in the middle and **150 mm (6 in)** at the edges, because gravity pulls the board down.

**How to do it:**

1. Keep screws **10–15 mm from the board edges** so the edges don't crumble.
2. Set screws at the same spot on boards that sit side by side, but don't put them directly across a joint from each other.
3. Start screwing from the centre of the board and work outward. This keeps the board flat against the studs.
4. Drive each screw so the head sits **just below the surface** but doesn't tear the paper. A torn screw hole won't hold, so put another screw about 50 mm away.
5. For 12.5 mm board on timber studs, use screws **32–38 mm long**.

**You'll need:**
- Drywall screws (coarse thread for timber studs, fine thread for metal studs)
- Drill-driver with a drywall dimpler bit, or a dedicated drywall screw gun
- Tape measure and pencil, or a chalk line to mark where the studs are
