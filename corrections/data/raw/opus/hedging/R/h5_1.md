On walls, put screws **every 300 mm along each stud**. On ceilings, close that up to **every 230 mm**, because the board's weight pulls down on the screws.

**How to fix it:**

1. **Check stud spacing.** Studs are usually at 400 mm or 600 mm centres. Board edges should always land on the middle of a stud.
2. **Keep screws in from the edges.** Stay about 10 mm from the paper-wrapped long edges and 13 mm from cut ends. Closer than that and the board can crumble.
3. **Work from the centre outwards.** Fix the middle studs first, then move to the edges. That keeps the board flat against the frame.
4. **Set the depth right.** Each screw head should sit just below the surface without tearing the paper. A torn screw hole doesn't hold, so put another screw about 50 mm away.
5. **Stagger screws on shared studs.** Where two boards meet on one stud, offset the screws so they don't line up.

**Screw length:** on timber studs, the screw should go at least 25 mm into the wood. That means 38 mm screws for 12.5 mm board and 42 mm for 15 mm board. On metal studs, use fine-thread drywall screws that come through the metal by at least 10 mm.

**You'll need:**
- Drywall screws (coarse thread for timber, fine thread for metal)
- A drill-driver with a drywall depth-setting bit, or a dedicated drywall screwdriver
- Tape measure and a pencil or chalk line to mark the stud lines
