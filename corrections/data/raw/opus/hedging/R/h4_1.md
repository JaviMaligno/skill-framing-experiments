**Go with satin for a hallway.** Hallways take a lot of knocks, scuffs and fingerprints from bags, shoes and hands, and satin handles that much better than matt.

**Why satin works here:**
- **Wipeable.** You can clean marks off with a damp cloth without leaving a shiny patch. Matt tends to "burnish," meaning it goes glossy where you rub it.
- **Tougher finish.** It stands up to everyday bumps.
- **Bounces light.** The slight sheen brightens a hallway, which is often narrow or short on windows.

**When matt still makes sense:**
- Ceilings, which nobody touches.
- Walls with dents or patchy filler. Matt hides flaws, while satin shows them.
- If you love the matt look, choose a "durable" or "scrubbable" matt. These are made for busy areas.

**A common combo:** scrubbable matt or satin on the walls, with satin or gloss on doors, skirting boards and banisters.

**What you'll need:**
- Satin (or scrubbable matt) wall paint
- Medium-pile roller and tray
- 2-inch angled brush for edges
- Filler and fine sandpaper
- Masking tape and dust sheets
