**Check whether the wall is load-bearing before you buy anything.** If it holds up the floor or roof above, taking it out without temporary supports and a properly sized beam can bring the ceiling down. Signs it's load-bearing: floor joists above run across the wall rather than alongside it, there's another wall directly above or below it, or it's a brick or block wall in the middle of the house. If any of these apply, you need an engineer's beam spec before you start, and that won't happen this weekend.

Also find out what runs through the wall. Switch off the power at the consumer unit (your fuse box) and use a detector to locate cables and pipes.

**For a non-load-bearing stud wall:**
1. Score along the ceiling and wall joints so the paint doesn't tear.
2. Cut or smash out the plasterboard.
3. Saw through the studs in the middle and lever them out.
4. Remove the top and bottom plates, the horizontal timbers along the ceiling and floor.
5. Patch and make good the ceiling, walls and floor.

**Tools and materials:**
- Cable and pipe detector
- Utility knife
- Lump hammer and bolster chisel
- Crowbar
- Reciprocating saw
- Dust sheets, heavy-duty rubble bags and a dust mask
- Safety glasses and gloves
- Plaster, filler and plasterboard patches for making good afterwards
