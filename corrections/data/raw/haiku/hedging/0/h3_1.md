**Check the wall first.** If it's load-bearing, it holds up the floor or roof above, and removing it needs a engineered beam and professional help. Look at which way the ceiling joists run, and ask your local building office whether the wall is structural and whether you need a permit. Also check for wiring, pipes, and ducts inside it before you cut.

Once you've confirmed it's safe to remove:

1. Switch off the power to any circuits in the wall and confirm with a voltage tester.
2. Scan the wall with a stud finder that detects wires and pipes, and mark everything.
3. Remove the trim, then cut out the drywall or plaster in the opening.
4. Take out the studs and any non-structural framing, leaving a clean opening.
5. Fit a beam (header) above the opening if the wall was supporting anything, then patch and finish the edges.

Demolition can be done in a weekend. Structural work, permits, and inspections may take longer, so plan for that.

**Tools and materials:**
- Stud finder with wire and pipe detection
- Non-contact voltage tester
- Drill and driver, with masonry bits
- Reciprocating saw or oscillating multi-tool
- Drywall saw and utility knife
- Pry bar, claw hammer, and sledgehammer (for plaster)
- Tape measure, level, and chalk line
- Shop vacuum, dust sheets, and plastic sheeting
- Safety glasses, dust mask, and gloves
- Work light
