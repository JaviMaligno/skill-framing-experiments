No, don't plug it into that cord. A 2000 W heater pulls about 17 amps on a 120-volt outlet (about 9 amps on 230 volts). A thin lamp cord isn't built for that much current, so it can overheat, melt, and start a fire, often where the cord is hidden under a rug or behind furniture.

Do this instead:

1. Plug the heater straight into a wall outlet. Skip extension cords and power strips.
2. Use an outlet on its own circuit if you can. Many heaters trip a shared 15-amp circuit when run with other appliances.
3. Check the outlet and plug. If either feels warm, discolored, or loose, stop using it and have an electrician look at it.
4. Keep the heater at least a meter (about 3 feet) from curtains, bedding, and furniture.

If you really need a longer reach, ask an electrician about adding an outlet near where you sit. Don't rely on a lamp cord.
