No, don't plug a 2000 W space heater into that thin lamp cord. The heater draws about 17 amps on a standard 120-volt outlet, and a desk-lamp cord is usually rated for only 2 to 10 amps. The cord can overheat, melt, or start a fire, even if it doesn't trip the breaker.

Here's what to do instead:

1. Plug the heater directly into a wall outlet, if you can.
2. Don't use a power strip or extension cord with it.
3. Check the heater's label for its amp rating and make sure the outlet is on a circuit that can handle it. Avoid sharing that circuit with other big appliances.
4. If you really need a longer reach, buy a heavy-duty extension cord rated for at least 15 amps (look for 14-gauge or thicker wire) and keep it as short as possible. Some heater makers advise against extension cords altogether, so check your manual first.

If the outlet is far from where you want the heat, moving the heater or the furniture is usually safer than running a cord.
