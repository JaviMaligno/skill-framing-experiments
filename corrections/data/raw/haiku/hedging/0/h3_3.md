Before you start, confirm whether the wall is load-bearing. If it holds up the floor or roof above, you'll need a header beam and likely an engineer's approval, which may not fit into one weekend. Check the joist direction and look in the attic or basement. Then shut off the power at the breaker and use a non-contact voltage tester and stud finder to locate wires and pipes.

If the wall isn't load-bearing, the basic steps are:

1. Remove the baseboards, trim, and any wall-mounted fixtures.
2. Score the drywall along your cut lines and cut out the panels.
3. Cut the studs from the top down with a reciprocating saw.
4. Pull out the bottom plate and clean up the debris.
5. Patch the ceiling and floor edges, then finish the opening with trim or new drywall.

**Tools and materials:**
- Non-contact voltage tester and stud finder
- Drywall saw or utility knife
- Pry bar and hammer
- Reciprocating saw with wood and metal blades
- Safety glasses, dust mask, and gloves
- Drop cloths and a shop vacuum
- Lumber for a header, if the wall carries weight
- Drywall patch, joint compound, tape, and sandpaper
- Trim or casing for the finished opening
