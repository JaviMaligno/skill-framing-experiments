Satin is usually the better choice for a hallway. It's tough enough to handle scuffs, fingerprints and the occasional wipe-down, and it still has a little sheen so it looks clean without being shiny.

Matt (flat) paint hides wall imperfections better and looks softer, but it's harder to clean. In a hallway, where shoulders, bags and dirty hands brush the walls, that matters more. Use matt only if the walls are in rough shape and you want to hide the flaws, or if the hallway gets very little traffic.

**Tips:**
- Choose a scrubbable or washable interior paint, and check that the label says it's for walls.
- If you're painting over a dark colour, a primer first will save you a second coat.
- Use an eggshell finish if you want something between matt and satin.

**What you'll need:**
- Interior wall paint in satin (or eggshell)
- Primer, if changing a dark colour
- Roller and tray, plus an angled brush for edges
- Painter's tape and drop cloths
- Filler and fine sandpaper for small dents
- Sponge or cloth for wiping walls before you start
