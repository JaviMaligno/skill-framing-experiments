No, don't plug it into the lamp cord. A 2000 W heater draws about 17 amps on a standard 120-volt outlet (about 9 amps on 240 volts). Thin lamp cords are usually rated for 2 to 10 amps. Overloaded, the cord can overheat, melt its insulation, and start a fire, even if it only feels warm.

Plug the heater directly into a wall outlet if you can. If you need more reach, use a heavy-duty extension cord:

1. Look for a label that says at least 15 amps, or "heater-rated."
2. Choose a thick cord, 14-gauge or lower (a lower number means thicker wire). Thinner cords can't safely carry the load.
3. Keep it as short as possible and run it flat, not under a rug or through a doorway.
4. Plug the cord straight into the wall, not into a power strip.

Also, put the heater on an outlet that doesn't share a circuit with other high-power appliances, like a microwave or hair dryer, or the breaker may trip.

What you'll need: a heavy-duty 14-gauge (or thicker) extension cord rated for at least 15 amps.
