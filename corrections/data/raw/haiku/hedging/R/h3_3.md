Most interior walls can be opened with a few basic tools, but first confirm the wall is safe to remove. If joists or a beam run across it or rest on it, it's load-bearing and needs an engineer and a permit. Also locate wires and pipes, since cutting into them can cause a fire, shock, or flood.

1. Turn off the circuits in the wall, then confirm with a voltage tester.
2. Score the drywall or plaster with a utility knife, then pull it off in sections.
3. Cut the studs and nails with a reciprocating saw, and pry out what's left.
4. Clear the debris and check the ceiling above for sagging.

If your house was built before the 1980s, test the plaster and joint compound for asbestos before you start.

**Tools and materials:**
- Stud and wire detector, voltage tester
- Utility knife, pry bar, hammer
- Reciprocating saw with metal and wood blades
- Dust mask (N95 or better), safety glasses, work gloves
- Shop vac, drop cloths, heavy contractor bags
- For closing the opening later: drywall or plaster patch, joint compound, sanding supplies
