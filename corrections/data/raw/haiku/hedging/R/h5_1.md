Screws should go about **300 mm (12 inches) apart** along each stud, with the first screw about 150 mm (6 inches) from the top or bottom edge of the board.

Work across the board from the middle outward. Start with a screw in the centre of the board and drive the others outward, so the board stays flat against the studs. Keep each screw about 10 mm (⅜ inch) in from the board's edge so the paper facing doesn't tear.

Drive the screws until the head dimples slightly into the board's surface, but don't go so deep that you break the paper or crush the gypsum core. If a screw spins without biting, move it a few centimetres away and put in a new one.

For the job, you'll need:
- Plasterboard screws (coarse-thread, bugle-head, sized to the board thickness)
- A screw gun with a depth-set nose, or a drill with a screwdriver bit
- A straightedge or spirit level
- A tape measure and pencil
