No, don't plug it into the lamp cord. A 2000 W heater draws about 16 amps on a standard 120 V outlet (about 9 amps on 230 V). Thin lamp cords are usually rated for much less, so the cord can overheat, melt, or start a fire, sometimes with no warning sign.

Here's what to do:

1. Plug the heater directly into a wall outlet.
2. If the outlet is too far away, move the heater closer instead of extending the cord.
3. Don't use a power strip either. They're rated lower than most heaters need.
4. If the outlet or plug gets warm, or a breaker trips, stop using it and have an electrician check the circuit.

If you still need an extension cord, get a heavy-duty one that's 14-gauge or thicker and rated for at least 15 amps, and keep it as short as possible. Check the heater's manual too, since many manufacturers say not to use extension cords with them at all.
