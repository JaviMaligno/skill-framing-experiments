For walls, space the screws about **300 mm (12 in)** apart along each stud. On ceilings, go closer, about **150–200 mm (6–8 in)**, because the board is working against gravity. Keep screws about 10 mm (3/8 in) in from the board edges and sink them so the head sits just below the paper surface without tearing it.

1. Mark the stud positions on the board with a pencil, using a stud finder if you can't see the framing.
2. Start the screws at the centre of the board and work outwards, so the board stays flat against the framing.
3. Keep the screws in line along each stud, and stagger the rows if the board is wider than one stud.
4. Drive each screw until the head is just below the surface. If the paper tears, you've gone too deep. If the screw spins without biting, the hole is stripped, so move it a few millimetres.

The one warning: before drilling, check for cables and pipes. Run the stud finder across the spot and, if you're unsure, look for a switch or socket box nearby, since cables usually run from it horizontally or vertically.

You'll need:
- Drywall screws (coarse thread, with a bugle or flat head)
- A drill with a clutch, or a screwdriver with a drywall bit
- A stud finder
- A pencil and a tape measure
