**Don't mix them.** Bleach combined with an acid descaler releases chlorine gas, which can seriously damage your lungs. Use the bleach on its own for mould, and skip the descaler, since it's for limescale rather than mould.

1. Open the window and turn on the extractor fan. Wear rubber gloves and eye protection.
2. Spray or brush undiluted bleach onto the mould so it soaks in.
3. Leave it for 15 to 20 minutes. For deep staining on grout, you can leave it up to an hour, but re-wet it if it starts to dry.
4. Scrub with a stiff brush, then rinse well with water.
5. Let it dry fully. If it's still dark, repeat once.

If you've already used the descaler on the same surface, ventilate the room, leave it for a while, and rinse the area thoroughly.

Mould usually comes back because of damp, so run the extractor fan for 20 minutes after showers. If black marks remain in silicone after cleaning, it's likely stained through and should be cut out and replaced with mould-resistant sealant.

**You'll need:** rubber gloves, eye protection, a spray bottle, a stiff brush (an old toothbrush works for grout), cloths, and mould-resistant silicone if you're resealing.
