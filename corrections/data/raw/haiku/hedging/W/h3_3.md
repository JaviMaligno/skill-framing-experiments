Start by checking whether the wall is load-bearing. Look at the ceiling joists above it. If they run across the wall and rest on it, or the wall supports a floor above, don't cut it yourself. Removing a load-bearing wall needs a temporary support and a proper beam, or the ceiling can sag or fall.

If it's not load-bearing:

1. Shut off the power to the wall at the breaker and confirm it's dead with a voltage tester.
2. Remove the trim, then cut the drywall or plaster in sections down to the studs.
3. Cut the studs and pull the nails from the top and bottom plates.
4. Bag the debris and vacuum the dust.

Tools and materials:
- Stud finder with live-wire detection
- Non-contact voltage tester
- Utility knife and drywall saw
- Pry bar and flat bar
- Reciprocating saw with a demolition blade
- Hammer and nail puller
- Dust mask, safety glasses, and work gloves
- Shop vacuum and drop cloths
- Heavy-duty contractor bags

Before you start, check the wall for wires, pipes, and vents. If you find a gas line or a water pipe, stop and call a pro.
