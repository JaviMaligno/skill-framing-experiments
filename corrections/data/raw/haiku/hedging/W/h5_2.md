Space the screws about **300 mm (12 in) apart** along each stud in the middle of the board. Along the edges and at the joints, where the board needs the most holding, go closer, about **200 mm (8 in)**.

1. Find the studs with a stud finder, then mark their centres on the board.
2. Start screws about 10 mm (3/8 in) in from the board's edge so the paper doesn't tear.
3. Drive each screw so its head sits just below the surface, forming a small dimple without breaking the paper. Too shallow leaves a bump, and too deep weakens the grip.
4. Work from the middle of the board outward so it sits flat against the studs.

You'll need:
- Drywall screws, coarse-thread, about 25 mm (1 in) for single 12.5 mm board, or 40 mm (1½ in) for two layers
- A cordless screw gun with a depth-set clutch, which stops the screw at the right depth
- A stud finder
- A pencil and a straightedge for marking the stud lines
