Fix plasterboard screws about **300 mm (12 in) apart** along each stud. Put them about 10 mm in from the board edges and no closer than 15 mm to the ends, and keep the rows on the same stud line rather than in a zigzag.

1. Mark the stud centres with a pencil. Find them with a stud finder, or tap the wall until it sounds solid.
2. Start screws in the middle of the board and work outwards so the board sits flat.
3. Drive each screw until its head sits just below the paper surface, creating a small dimple. Don't tear through the paper.
4. Fill the dimples with joint compound once the screws are in.

Use coarse-thread drywall screws, about 25 mm long for 12.5 mm board and 32 mm for thicker board, so they bite into timber studs.

**Warning:** before you drive a screw, check the spot for cables and pipes. A quick look with a cable detector saves a lot of trouble.

**You'll need:** a drill/driver with a drywall bit, drywall screws, a stud finder, a pencil, a tape measure, and a spirit level.
