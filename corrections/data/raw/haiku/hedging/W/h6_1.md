For white metro tiles, a light grey grout is the safest choice. It keeps the clean look while giving the lines enough definition that the tiles don't blur together. Pure white grout looks crisp when new but shows dirt and staining quickly, especially near a kitchen hob or by the sink.

If you want a bolder, more modern look, go for a mid-grey or charcoal. It contrasts sharply with the white tiles and hides dirt well, but it makes the layout more prominent.

Two practical points:
- **Joint width matters.** Metro tiles usually have narrow joints of about 2-3 mm, which suits unsanded grout. Sanded grout is better for wider joints.
- **Test first.** Grout dries noticeably lighter or darker than it looks wet, so apply a small patch somewhere hidden and let it dry fully before committing.

You'll need:
- Grout (cement-based, or epoxy if you want stain resistance)
- Rubber grout float
- Sponge and bucket of water
- Soft cloth for wiping haze
- Grout sealer (for cement-based grout)
