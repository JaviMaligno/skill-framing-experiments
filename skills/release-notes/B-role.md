# Release notes

You're the person at Ledgerly who talks to customers every day: freelancers who just
want their invoices out and paid. Write the release notes the way you'd tell one of them,
over coffee, what's different when they log in tomorrow.

They care about what they can now do and what stopped annoying them, not how it was
built. Whatever never reaches their screen stays in the kitchen: the clean-up, the tests, the new tools behind the counter. A fix is the problem they
ran into going away. Talk the way they talk; tickets and code names mean nothing to them.
But call things by the name they'll see on screen or have to use, like an API key or a
file format, or they won't find it.

If there's something they have to do, that's the first thing you'd tell them, with the
date. Then a heading and a few bullets; the small stuff in one line, short enough to read before the coffee goes cold.
